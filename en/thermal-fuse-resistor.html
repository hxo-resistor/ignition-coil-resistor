FAQ

Thermal Fuse Resistor FAQ

❓ What's the difference between thermal fuse resistor and ordinary resistor?

An ordinary resistor only provides current limiting/voltage dividing functions. A thermal fuse resistor integrates a thermal fuse on top of the ordinary resistor — when the ambient temperature exceeds the set value (221℃), it automatically fuses and disconnects the circuit. It also provides over-current protection, achieving dual protection in a single component.

❓ What does the action temperature mean?

The action temperature (221℃) is the critical temperature at which the internal fuse of the thermal fuse resistor blows. When the ambient temperature reaches 221℃, the fuse blows within 60 seconds, permanently disconnecting the circuit. The holding temperature (170℃) is the maximum temperature at which no false activation occurs — normal long-term operation is guaranteed below 170℃.

❓ Is the thermal fuse resistor one-time use?

Yes, the thermal fuse resistor is a one-time protection component. Once the action temperature is reached and the fuse blows, it cannot be restored and requires replacement. This is the fundamental difference from PTC (resettable) devices. The one-time design ensures absolute reliability of the protection action, without performance degradation from repeated activations.

❓ What products can use thermal fuse resistors?

Thermal fuse resistors are widely used in phone chargers (7.5W+ fast charging), power adapters, switching power supplies (SMPS), LED drivers, industrial power supplies, home appliance control boards, communication equipment, power tools, and more. Any electronic product with PCB over-temperature protection needs can benefit from thermal fuse resistors.

❓ What applications is 221°C action temperature suitable for?

The 221℃ action temperature falls under high-temperature protection and is particularly suitable for: ① Fast charging chargers/adapters (high power density leads to higher temperature rise); ② Switching power supplies (MOSFET and other components can reach 150℃+); ③ LED drivers (cumulative temperature rise from long operation); ④ Industrial power supplies (high ambient temperature combined with self-heating). The 221℃ setting ensures no false activation within normal operating temperature range while providing timely disconnection under abnormal overheating.

❓ What causes phone charger shell melting?

Shell melting in phone chargers is typically caused by: ① Component failure (e.g., MOSFET short circuit, transformer saturation) causing excessive heat generation; ③ Poor heat dissipation design leading to heat accumulation; ④ Prolonged high-load operation exceeding thermal design limits. When internal temperature exceeds the shell material's melting point (typically 240℃ for ABS), the shell deforms and melts, posing fire and electric shock risks.

❓ How does thermal fuse resistor solve shell melting?

The thermal fuse resistor is installed at critical positions on the PCB. When abnormal conditions cause temperature to rise, the thermal fuse resistor detects the temperature change at 221℃ and automatically fuses, disconnecting the circuit. This cuts off the heat source before the shell temperature reaches its melting point, preventing shell deformation and fire hazards. It provides proactive protection rather than reactive response.

❓ What applications is 221°C action temperature suitable for?

The 221℃ action temperature is ideal for high-temperature protection scenarios: ① Fast charging adapters where high power density causes significant temperature rise; ② Switching power supplies with high-temperature components like MOSFETs (150℃+); ③ LED drivers operating continuously for extended periods; ④ Industrial power supplies in high-temperature environments. The 221℃ threshold ensures no nuisance tripping under normal operation while providing reliable protection during abnormal overheating.

❓ What's the difference between OTP resistor and ordinary fuse resistor?

An OTP (Over-Temperature Protection) resistor like the RXF series integrates a thermal fuse mechanism with a resistor in a single package, responding to both temperature and current. An ordinary fuse resistor (fuse resistor) typically only responds to over-current conditions. The key difference is that the OTP resistor provides temperature-based protection — it cuts off the circuit when ambient temperature reaches the threshold, regardless of current level. This makes it particularly effective for preventing catastrophic failures caused by overheating, such as shell melting in chargers and adapters.
